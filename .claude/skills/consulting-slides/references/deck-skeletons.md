# Deck skeletons

Starting points for the storyline, not templates to fill in. Cut any slide
that does not advance the argument.

## Recommendation deck (the default)

1. The recommendation, in one sentence, with the ask
2. Why now — the trigger or the decision deadline
3. What we found — three to five findings, one slide each, each with its
   exhibit
4. What it means — the implication of the findings taken together
5. Options considered, and why this one
6. Risks and what would change our mind
7. What we need from you — decision, owner, date

## Board paper

1. Executive summary — the decision, the recommendation, the ask
2. Performance against plan — the bridge by driver
3. The two or three things that moved
4. Outlook and the revised forecast
5. Decisions required

## Investment committee screener

1. The opportunity in one page — what, size, why us
2. Market: size, growth, and the structure of it
3. Company: what it does and how it makes money
4. Financial profile and the key drivers
5. The thesis — three value creation levers
6. Key risks and the diligence that would retire them
7. Process, timing, and the ask

## Commercial due diligence findings

1. Headline verdict and the confidence behind it
2. Market sizing — top-down and bottom-up, side by side
3. Growth drivers and their evidence
4. Competitive position — scored against stated criteria
5. Customer evidence — retention, cohorts, concentration
6. Risks to the thesis
7. Implications for the deal

## Working session pack

1. Objective of the session and what we need to leave with
2. Where we got to last time
3. The question on the table
4. The evidence, one slide per strand
5. Options, with the trade-offs made explicit
6. Next steps, owners, dates

## Rules for all of them

- The first slide carries the answer. Consultants do not build to a reveal.
- Appendix anything that supports but does not advance. Appendices can be
  long; the main body cannot.
- If a section has one slide, it is not a section.
